Before your interview
Complete every item on this list. These are requirements, not suggestions.
Log on 5 minutes before the scheduled time Required
Be at your computer and connected to the interview link 5 minutes before the start time. Use those minutes to confirm your camera and microphone are working correctly. Joining late will negatively impact your evaluation.
Camera on — no exceptions Required
This is a camera-on interview. Your video must be enabled from start to finish. If you experience a technical issue, notify the interviewer immediately via the Teams chat. Interviews will not proceed with your camera off.
Use a computer, not your phone Required
You must take this interview from a laptop or desktop computer. The client needs to see your body language and assess your professional presence — this is not possible from a mobile phone. A phone interview will not be accepted.
Install Microsoft Teams before the day Required
Download and install the Microsoft Teams desktop app before your interview day — not on the morning of. Do not rely on the browser version. Test your audio and video inside Teams at least 24 hours in advance.
Find a quiet, private space Required
Even if you are at home, you must be in a quiet room with no interruptions for the duration of the call. Close the door. Use a desk or table with a plain, neutral background — clutter, beds, sofas, or public spaces are not appropriate. The room must be well-lit so your face is clearly visible.
Dress in business attire Important
Dress as you would for an in-person interview — business or smart casual from head to toe. Being at home is not a reason to dress casually. Pyjamas, graphic t-shirts, or loungewear are not appropriate and will be noted by the interviewer.
Eliminate all distractions Required
Before the call begins: silence your phone and put it out of reach, mute all desktop notifications, close unrelated tabs, and let people in your home know not to interrupt. During the interview, do not eat, smoke, watch anything, or hold side conversations. Your full attention must be on the interviewer.
2
How to answer interview questions: the STAR method
A simple structure that makes any answer clear, compelling, and memorable.
S
Situation
Set the scene. Give just enough context for the interviewer to understand the challenge or scenario you faced.
T
Task
Describe your specific role or responsibility in that situation. What was expected of you?
A
Action
Explain exactly what you did — not your team, not your manager. Focus on your individual contribution and decisions.
R
Result
Share the outcome. Quantify it where possible — numbers, percentages, timeframes. What changed because of your actions?
Example answer using STAR
S
Situation: At my previous role, our team was managing 3 client accounts simultaneously and missed two deadlines in a single month due to poor task visibility.
T
Task: As the team lead, it was my responsibility to identify the root cause and implement a fix before the next delivery cycle.
A
Action: I introduced a shared Kanban board, set up weekly check-ins, and created a deadline alert system using our project management tool so nothing fell through the gaps.
R
Result: We delivered 100% of projects on time for the following quarter, and client satisfaction scores improved from 7.2 to 9.1 out of 10.
Prepare 4–5 STAR stories before your interview. Think of situations where you solved a problem, led a team, handled conflict, met a tight deadline, or went above and beyond. These stories will cover most behavioural questions you'll be asked.
Be yourself. Interviewers are not looking for a perfect script — they are looking for the real you. Speak naturally, use your own words, and share stories that genuinely reflect how you work. Authenticity is always more memorable than a rehearsed performance.
